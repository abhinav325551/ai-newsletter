## Models & Research This week's most consequential AI research story isn't a new model drop — it's a security reckoning. Across coding agents, the attack surface has proven to be credential infrastructure, not model weights, exposing a structural gap between how these tools are evaluated (benchmark performance) and how they fail in production (IAM and token hygiene). Meanwhile, the research frontier is quietly expanding beyond language into spatiotemporal world modeling for physical systems.

## SaaS Disruption Watch The week's dominant signal isn't a single product launch — it's a structural shift in *where* software value accrues. OpenAI CFO Sarah Friar's "vertical wall of demand" comment is less a growth story than a displacement warning: when foundation model providers capture end-user workflows directly, the middleware SaaS layer — the Zendeskss, the Intercom, the Notion-alikes — faces a squeeze from both above and below. Coding agents "breaking containment" into knowledge work and creative work isn't a metaphor; it's a description of category collapse happening in real time.
--- • **[Latent Space] OpenAI Codex targeting knowledge work, Anthropic Claude targeting creative work** — The thesis that coding agents are "breaking containment" into general knowledge work directly pressures incumbents like Notion, Confluence, and Coda: if Codex can draft, organize, and execute knowledge workflows autonomously, the "connected workspace" SaaS category loses its core value proposition to an agentic layer that doesn't require a separate subscription. [Link](https://www.latent.space/p/ainews-agents-for-everything-else) • **[Latent Space] Claude cited as primary challenger in creative and long-form knowledge work** — Anthropic's Claude is increasingly positioned against incumbents like Grammarly, Jasper, and Adobe's content tools — not by matching their feature sets, but by eliminating the need for category-specific SaaS entirely; a general-purpose model handling creative briefs, editing, and ideation collapses what were once distinct SKUs into a single API call. [Link](https://www.latent.space/p/ainews-agents-for-everything-else) • **[Bloomberg Technology] OpenAI CFO Sarah Friar reports "vertical wall of demand" for OpenAI products** — The direct-to-enterprise push implied by Friar's demand characterization puts OpenAI in explicit competition with vertical SaaS players in legal tech (Harvey's turf), customer support (Salesforce Einstein, Intercom Fin), and financial analysis (Bloomberg Terminal add-ons, Tegus) — categories where OpenAI's o-series reasoning models are capable enough to undercut specialized incumbents on price-per-task. [Link](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-05-01/openai-finance-chief-sees-vertical-wall-of-demand-for-products) • **[Bloomberg Technology] AI debt investors show fatigue after $300 billion binge** — The credit market cooldown is a secondary disruption vector for SaaS: AI infrastructure companies that were financing compute expansion at scale may face capital constraints, which could slow the rate of challenger deployment — giving incumbents like ServiceNow, Workday, and SAP a narrower but real window to embed AI features before pure-play AI competitors achieve distribution parity. [Link](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-04-30/ai-debt-investors-show-signs-of-fatigue-after-300-billion-binge) --- **The through-line this week:** The disruption mechanism is no longer "AI startup builds a better point solution." It's "foundation model providers absorb entire SaaS categories as agent use cases." Notion, Grammarly, Intercom, and niche vertical SaaS players without defensible proprietary data or workflow lock-in are the most exposed.
