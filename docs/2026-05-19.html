# The Big Thing: China's Dexterous Robotics Race Is Heading to Public Markets Linkerbot, a Chinese startup specializing in dexterous robotic hands, is reportedly considering an IPO on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange, according to people with knowledge of the matter cited by Bloomberg. The move — still in consideration and not formally announced — would make Linkerbot one of the first pure-play dexterous manipulation companies to test public market appetite, at a moment when the gap between robot perception and robot *dexterity* has become the defining bottleneck in humanoid and industrial robotics deployment. If you've been watching where the real engineering difficulty sits in the embodied AI stack, it's here: getting a machine to reliably grasp, rotate, and manipulate objects the way a human hand does remains an unsolved problem at scale. The context matters. China has aggressively positioned its robotics sector as a strategic priority, with state guidance pushing domestic manufacturers to reduce dependence on foreign components across the full hardware stack. Dexterous end-effectors — the hands and fingers that interface with the physical world — have historically been dominated by a small number of European and Japanese suppliers. Linkerbot is among a cohort of Chinese startups, alongside names like Inspire-Robots and FFTAI, racing to build domestically competitive alternatives. Hong Kong, rather than the mainland A-share markets or a US listing, is a deliberate choice given the current geopolitical environment: it offers international capital access without the regulatory exposure of a US exchange listing for a Chinese hardware company operating in a sector with obvious dual-use implications. The broader implication is that the embodied AI investment cycle is maturing fast enough to support public listings, not just private funding rounds. Until recently, the dexterous robotics space was almost entirely pre-revenue or early-revenue, sustained by venture capital and strategic corporate investment from the large humanoid robot developers (BYD, Unitree, Agility's backers) who need reliable hand suppliers. A successful Linkerbot IPO would function as a pricing event for the entire dexterous manipulation subsector — giving investors, acquirers, and competitors a public comparable for the first time. It would also accelerate consolidation pressure on smaller players who can't access equivalent capital. What to watch: whether Linkerbot files a formal prospectus with the Hong Kong Stock Exchange Commission and what revenue and margin figures it discloses — that data will be the first hard look at unit economics in the dexterous hardware space. Also watch whether the IPO filing triggers a response from US policymakers scrutinizing Chinese robotics companies' access to international capital markets, a dynamic that has already affected semiconductor and drone-adjacent firms. The timing of any listing relative to the humanoid robot product cycles at companies like Figure, Physical Intelligence, and 1X will also signal whether Western players move to lock in domestic supply chains before a Chinese dexterous hardware supplier achieves public-market scale. *(IPO plans unconfirmed; sourced from anonymous Bloomberg sources.)*
