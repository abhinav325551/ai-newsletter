## The Big Thing: a16z Books Two Landmark AI Exits in a Single Week The most important story in AI right now isn't a new model release or a safety breakthrough — it's what just happened to Andreessen Horowitz's balance sheet. According to Newcomer, a16z's infrastructure practice scored two major exits in the same week: Cursor, the AI-powered code editor, and OpenRouter, the API aggregation platform that lets developers route queries across competing frontier models. The simultaneity matters as much as the substance. Landing two liquidity events of this magnitude in a single week is the kind of outcome that validates an entire fund thesis, not just individual bets. To understand why this is significant, you need to understand a16z's megafund strategy. Rather than spreading modest capital across dozens of early-stage bets and waiting for one to break out, the megafund model concentrates large checks into infrastructure plays that sit beneath multiple AI winners — the "picks and shovels" layer. Cursor benefits every developer regardless of which LLM wins the model wars. OpenRouter benefits every application builder who wants to avoid vendor lock-in. These aren't directional bets on GPT-4 versus Claude — they're structural bets on the proliferation of AI tooling itself. That thesis just paid out twice in seven days. The broader implication cuts directly against the narrative that AI infrastructure is overcrowded and undifferentiated. Cursor competed against GitHub Copilot — a Microsoft-backed product with enormous distribution — and won meaningful enough market share to generate a venture-scale exit. OpenRouter, meanwhile, built durable value precisely because the model landscape fragmented rather than consolidated. Both outcomes suggest that developers are willing to pay for workflow improvements and optionality even when free or subsidized alternatives exist. That's a meaningful signal about where enterprise AI spending is actually landing, as opposed to where it's projected to land. What to watch next: Newcomer notes that OpenAI and Anthropic are showing strong revenue figures but face unresolved complications ahead of their planned IPOs — the nature of those hang-ups isn't fully detailed in the available sourcing (unconfirmed specifics). If the megafund model continues producing exits at this pace, expect LPs to push a16z toward an even larger subsequent fund, and expect competitors at Sequoia — currently topping the leaderboard for investments in newly minted unicorns per Newcomer — to accelerate their own infrastructure deployment. The real question isn't whether AI infrastructure creates value; it's whether the current entry valuations for the next generation of Cursor-style companies already price in the outcome.
