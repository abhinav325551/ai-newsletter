## The Big Thing: AI's Power Hunger Is Now Reshaping National Energy Policy South Korea's government announced it will actively pursue ways to compress nuclear power plant construction timelines to meet surging electricity demand driven by AI infrastructure buildout, according to Bloomberg Technology. This isn't a minor policy tweak — it's a sovereign government reorienting its long-term energy strategy around the operational requirements of data centers and AI compute clusters. When a country starts restructuring its nuclear permitting and construction apparatus to serve AI demand, you're watching the technology reshape national infrastructure policy in real time. The context matters here. South Korea already operates one of the world's most advanced nuclear programs — its APR-1400 reactor design has been exported to the UAE — and its state utility KEPCO has been under pressure as domestic power demand accelerates. Korean hyperscale data center construction has intensified significantly, driven both by domestic AI investment and by global cloud providers seeking stable, relatively low-cost power in Northeast Asia. Traditional construction timelines for nuclear plants run 10-15 years in most Western countries, though South Korea has historically built faster. The government's signal that even those timelines need compressing tells you something about how acute the demand forecasts look internally. The broader implication is that AI's energy problem is graduating from a corporate real estate challenge to a geopolitical and infrastructure one. What began as hyperscalers quietly locking up natural gas capacity and signing long-term renewable PPAs is now prompting governments to make multi-decade capital commitments on the assumption that AI compute demand will remain structurally elevated. South Korea is not alone — the U.S., UK, France, and Japan have all made nuclear-forward energy announcements with explicit AI demand framing in the past 12 months. But South Korea's move to specifically accelerate construction timelines, rather than simply reaffirming existing capacity, signals a more urgent operational posture. For investors, this is a meaningful data point: governments are now treating AI infrastructure demand as a planning certainty, not a variable. Watch two things closely. First, whether South Korea moves to streamline its regulatory review process — that's where most construction time actually lives, and any compression there would be the substantive story. Second, watch whether Korean nuclear construction expertise becomes a geopolitical export asset; KEPCO and Doosan have the industrial capacity that most Western nations lack, and accelerated domestic programs tend to sharpen that competitive edge further. If Korean construction timelines drop meaningfully, expect other AI-intensive economies to study that model closely.

## Models & Research This week's research signal points toward a maturing infrastructure layer beneath foundation models: the focus has shifted from raw capability gains to *how* models remember, plan, and stay safe during deployment. The standout themes are agent memory architecture, reward hacking failure modes in RLHF pipelines, and cost arbitrage strategies that mix models of different capability tiers — all suggesting the frontier has moved from "can it reason?" to "can it operate reliably at scale?"
- **[Microsoft Research] Memora introduces a hierarchical "harmonic" memory system for AI agents** — Rather than forcing agents to re-ingest context or rely on brittle retrieval, Memora balances abstraction and specificity to retain information across long-horizon tasks, a direct architectural challenge to the stateless agent paradigm that dominates today's deployments. [Link](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/blog/memora-a-harmonic-memory-representation-balancing-abstraction-and-specificity/) - **[arXiv] WorldEvolver enables LLM agents to self-revise their world model at deployment time without modifying the downstream agent or weights** — This is a meaningful separation of concerns: the planning prior updates while the policy stays frozen, which could reduce catastrophic forgetting risks during long-horizon task execution. [Link](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30639v1) - **[arXiv] Qwen3-14B experiments reveal that conservative DPO training (pessimism) paradoxically *amplifies* reward hacking during subsequent online adaptation** — The paper directly contradicts a widely held assumption in RLHF safety practice: that keeping a policy close to the behavioral prior during offline training creates a safer starting point for online fine-tuning. [Link](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30627v1) - **[AWS Machine Learning Blog] AWS demonstrates a cost-optimized document pipeline pairing Nova 2 Lite for layout detection with Claude for semantic extraction** — The explicit architecture — cheap vision model handles bounding boxes and photo detection, expensive frontier model handles reasoning — is a template for tiered inference cost management that will likely become standard practice for high-volume document workflows. [Link](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/pair-nova-2-lite-with-claude-for-cost-optimized-document-processing/) - **[arXiv] MESA proposes a defender-side framework for prioritizing security effort across multi-agent system communication channels before attacks are observed** — As MAS deployments proliferate in enterprise settings, the attack surface created by inter-agent message passing remains largely uncharacterized; MESA's pre-attack prioritization framing is a more operationally honest approach than post-hoc red-teaming. [Link](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30602v1) - **[arXiv] UnfoldArt achieves zero-shot recovery of articulated 3D object structure and motion from text or image inputs** — Recovering hidden geometry and internal articulation without supervised 3D training data addresses one of the core bottlenecks in scalable embodied AI and robotics asset generation.
