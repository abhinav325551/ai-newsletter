## 1. The Big Thing

## THE BIG THING

### The AI Chip Rally Is Cracking — and the Contagion Is Going Global

The semiconductor selloff that began in U.S. markets has now spread to South Korea's Kospi, signaling that what looked like isolated profit-taking may be something more systematic. South Korean chipmakers — most critically Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix, which together dominate global HBM (high-bandwidth memory) supply — are caught directly in the blast radius. HBM is the memory architecture that makes high-performance AI inference and training possible at scale, meaning this isn't a peripheral story about speculative tech stocks. It's a stress test on the physical supply chain underpinning the entire AI buildout.

The context here matters. AI-linked chip stocks have been on an extraordinary run, driven almost entirely by the narrative that hyperscaler capex — from Microsoft, Google, Amazon, and Meta — would sustain semiconductor demand indefinitely. That narrative encouraged valuations that priced in years of flawless execution. What Bloomberg's reporting now reflects is the market beginning to question the timing mismatch: hyperscalers are spending aggressively, but revenue models that justify that spending at the application layer remain thin, delayed, or unproven. When the gap between infrastructure investment and monetization widens, equities that priced in the optimistic scenario correct — sometimes sharply.

The Korean market's involvement is analytically significant because it closes a loop. U.S. chip names like Nvidia carry obvious AI valuation premiums, so their selloffs can be read as sentiment-driven. But South Korean exposure is more fundamental — SK Hynix in particular is Nvidia's primary HBM3E supplier, and Samsung is fighting to qualify its own HBM for Nvidia's next-generation platforms. When those stocks fall in sympathy, you're watching the market reprice not just enthusiasm but actual near-term order expectations. That's a different and more serious signal.

Watch three things from here: first, whether the selloff is arrested by any hyperscaler earnings guidance or capex commitment that reasserts demand conviction — the next major test is earnings season. Second, whether SK Hynix's HBM qualification pipeline for Nvidia's Blackwell successor remains on schedule, since any slip there would validate the bearish read. Third, watch whether this spreads further into TSMC's Taiwan-listed shares, which would confirm that the market is repricing the entire AI semiconductor stack rather than just memory. If TSMC holds, this may be a rotation. If it doesn't, you're looking at a broader recalibration of how markets are valuing the AI infrastructure thesis.

## 9. Papers of the Week

## Papers of the Week

This week's preprints cluster around a shared tension: systems that are impressive in controlled settings but brittle or biased when deployed against the messiness of real data and real users. Two papers push the frontier of 3D scene representation, one tackles the persistent demographic bias problem in generative image models, and one takes a genuinely novel angle on LLM memory by borrowing from cognitive science rather than systems engineering. The memory paper is the most conceptually ambitious of the batch.


- **[arXiv] AutoMem treats memory management as a learnable cognitive skill, not a hardcoded system** — Rather than bolting on retrieval modules, AutoMem promotes file-system operations (read, write, delete) to first-class actions that the model trains to use alongside task actions, operationalizing the cognitive science concept of metamemory—knowing *what* to encode and *when* to retrieve—directly in LLM training. [Link](http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01224v1)

- **[arXiv] "World from Motion" generates freely renderable dynamic 3D Gaussians from a single monocular video** — The method conditions a video diffusion model on pixel-aligned renderings that encode appearance, geometry, and 3D scene motion simultaneously, using that conditioning to correct artifacts and hallucinate occluded regions that a naive reconstruction would simply miss. [Link](http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01202v1)

- **[arXiv] Learned 3D Gaussian representations outperform implicit neural representations for volumetric compression of unstructured data** — Existing INR-based compressors for unstructured volumes still require storing partial mesh geometry to enable later sampling, which caps achievable compression ratios; this paper sidesteps that by encoding geometry directly into the Gaussian representation. [Link](http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01164v1)

- **[arXiv] EquiSteer corrects demographic bias in text-to-image diffusion models via cross-attention steering at inference time** — On canonical skewed prompts like "a photo of a CEO" or "a photo of a nurse," existing debiasing methods require retraining or fine-tuning; EquiSteer intervenes in the cross-attention mechanism without weight updates, which matters practically given the cost of retraining frontier diffusion models. [Link](http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01147v1)
