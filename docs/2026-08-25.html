## The Big Thing: Anthropic Puts $5M Behind AI Wellbeing Measurement — And the Industry Should Pay Attention Anthropic announced a $5 million grant program this week specifically targeting independent research into how AI systems affect user wellbeing. The program goes beyond funding alone: grantees receive direct API access to Anthropic's models and technical support, with a hard requirement that all resulting evaluations be open-source. The explicit goal is to build measurement infrastructure — standardized, reproducible benchmarks that any lab could use to assess whether their models are helping or harming the people interacting with them. This is not a corporate wellness initiative. It's an attempt to solve a genuine gap in the field's evaluation toolkit. The context matters here. Current AI benchmarks are overwhelmingly capability-focused — MMLU, HumanEval, GPQA, and their variants tell you what a model *can do*, not what it *does to you*. Questions about dependency formation, emotional manipulation risk, displacement of human connection, and long-term cognitive effects remain almost entirely unmeasured at scale. Anthropic has been unusually public about taking these concerns seriously — Claude's system-level design explicitly references avoiding fostering unhealthy reliance — but the company has also acknowledged internally that it lacks robust tools to verify whether those design choices actually work. This grant program is, in part, an admission of that gap. The implications extend well past Anthropic's own model lineup. By requiring open-source outputs, Anthropic is structurally preventing grantees from producing proprietary evaluations that only benefit one lab. If this produces credible, widely-adopted measurement frameworks, it creates a shared standard that regulators, journalists, and rival labs can all apply — including to Anthropic itself. That's a meaningful constraint to voluntarily accept. It also subtly repositions the wellbeing measurement problem as a pre-competitive infrastructure challenge, similar to how interpretability research has been framed, rather than a differentiator any single company should own. What you should watch: who Anthropic actually funds. The credibility of this entire effort hinges on whether the grantees are genuinely independent researchers — academic psychologists, behavioral economists, public health scholars — or whether the program gravitates toward AI-adjacent organizations already aligned with Anthropic's worldview. Watch also for whether OpenAI, Google DeepMind, or Meta AI feel pressure to either adopt resulting evaluation frameworks or launch competing programs. If the open-source evaluations gain traction, they could become de facto industry standards before any regulatory body gets around to mandating them — which would represent a significant first-mover advantage in shaping how "responsible AI" gets operationally defined.
