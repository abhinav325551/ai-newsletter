## THE BIG THING ### ASML Is the Most Undervalued Chokepoint in AI Infrastructure — And the Market Is Only Just Noticing ASML hit fresh record highs this week, but the more important signal is buried beneath the headline: the Dutch lithography monopolist is trading at its cheapest forward price-to-earnings ratio relative to major semiconductor peers in over a decade, according to Bloomberg's Dasha Afanasieva reporting from Amsterdam. That disconnect matters because ASML isn't a peripheral player in the AI buildout — it is the singular, non-substitutable supplier of extreme ultraviolet (EUV) lithography machines required to manufacture leading-edge chips at 7nm and below. Every NVIDIA H100, every custom AI ASIC from Google, Amazon, and Microsoft, every TSMC wafer feeding the current AI supercycle runs through machines that only ASML can produce. A company with that level of structural leverage trading at a relative valuation discount isn't a curiosity — it's an anomaly demanding explanation. The context here is critical. ASML's recent performance has been shaped by two countervailing forces: genuine demand acceleration driven by hyperscaler AI capex commitments running into the hundreds of billions, and persistent overhang from U.S. export controls that have progressively restricted ASML's ability to sell even older DUV (deep ultraviolet) systems into China — historically a significant revenue contributor. The Dutch government, acting under U.S. pressure, has curtailed ASML's China export licenses since 2023, and Washington has continued tightening those restrictions. That regulatory risk has weighed on sentiment even as the underlying Western demand picture strengthened materially. Investors appear to be discounting ASML for the China exposure it's losing rather than pricing the monopoly infrastructure position it retains everywhere else. The strategic implications for the AI landscape are significant and underappreciated. The entire AI infrastructure narrative has been dominated by discussion of model companies, GPU allocations, and hyperscaler spending — but the actual physical constraint on AI scaling sits one layer deeper, at the fab equipment level. TSMC cannot simply spin up new leading-edge capacity on demand; each new fab requires years of lead time and a queue of ASML EUV tools that the company produces at a rate of roughly 20 high-NA units per year at full ramp. If you're modeling the ceiling on AI compute availability over the next three to five years, ASML's production throughput is a harder constraint than NVIDIA's packaging capacity or TSMC's wafer starts. The relative valuation discount suggests institutional money hasn't fully internalized this bottleneck dynamic, or is still anchoring to the China revenue haircut as the dominant variable. What to watch: ASML's next earnings call for any revision to EUV shipment guidance and backlog figures — the backlog number is the most honest leading indicator of where leading-edge semiconductor capacity is heading 18–24 months out. Also watch for any executive commentary on high-NA EUV adoption timelines; Intel, TSMC, and Samsung are all in early-stage deployment of high-NA tools, and acceleration there would be a direct signal that the next node transition is pulling forward. Finally, monitor U.S.-Netherlands diplomatic channels around export control scope — any further restrictions on DUV shipments to China could trigger another sentiment reset, while any relaxation (unlikely under current policy trajectory) would be an immediate catalyst. The valuation gap Bloomberg is flagging is either a genuine mispricing or a rational discount for geopolitical risk that isn't going away. Determining which is the right frame is the most important analytical question in semiconductor infrastructure right now.

## Models & Research The week's model releases reveal a clear industry push toward **unified, encoder-free multimodal architectures** — a design philosophy that trades specialized components for leaner, more deployable systems. Google DeepMind and academic researchers are converging on discrete token representations as the unifying substrate for vision-language models, while Anthropic's Mythos-class launch introduces a new tier of frontier capability paired with immediately contentious usage restrictions. On the research side, the question is shifting from *can models do X* to *how do we steer and adapt them reliably at test time*.
--- - **[Google DeepMind] Gemma 4 12B is the first mid-sized model in the Gemma 4 family — encoder-free, natively multimodal, and positioned explicitly for laptop deployment, slotting between the E4B edge model and the 27B MoE.** The encoder-free design is the architectural signal worth watching: it collapses the traditional vision encoder + language model stack into a single unified backbone, a bet that scale can substitute for specialization. [Link](https://deepmind.google/blog/introducing-gemma-4-12b-a-unified-encoder-free-multimodal-model/) - **[Latent Space / AINews] Anthropic's Claude "Fable 5" (Mythos-class) launched approximately 63 days after the original Mythos announcement, with Opus 4.8 — itself barely two weeks old — already reportedly leading on some benchmarks at time of release (unconfirmed exact benchmark citations).** The launch was immediately overshadowed by controversial usage policy terms, a pattern suggesting Anthropic is tightening acceptable-use constraints as capability ceilings rise. [Link](https://www.latent.space/p/ainews-anthropic-claude-fable-5-mythos) - **[arXiv] ARM introduces a discrete semantic visual tokenizer that unifies image understanding, generation, and editing inside a single next-token prediction autoregressive framework**, trained with multiple joint objectives to enforce both semantic discriminability and visual fidelity. This is a direct architectural challenge to diffusion-based image generation pipelines and CLIP-style dual-encoder understanding models. [Link](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11188v1) - **[arXiv] A new "phase diagram" framework for multimodal learning identifies the precise conditions under which cross-modal alignment (CA) outperforms cross-modal prediction (CP), and vice versa** — a gap in systematic understanding that has left practitioners in scientific domains like biomedicine and astrophysics largely guessing. The practical payoff is a principled method for choosing training paradigms when instruments and data modalities are heterogeneous. [Link](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11190v1) - **[arXiv] EEVEE is the first multi-dataset test-time prompt learning framework for LLM agents**, designed to handle heterogeneous input streams across multiple domains simultaneously — existing methods are single-dataset only, making them brittle in real-world deployment where task distributions shift continuously. [Link](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11182v1) - **[arXiv] New steering research argues that current test-time representation interventions on large reasoning models (LRMs) fail because they rely on features that detect *already-generated* behavior rather than *predicting future* behavioral outcomes**, proposing that predictive internal features enable materially better output steering without the quality degradation seen in prior work. ## SaaS Disruption Watch The most honest signal this week isn't a product launch or a funding round — it's a headcount table. Shopee's mass developer layoffs illustrate the mechanism that SaaS incumbents have been dreading: AI doesn't just compete with software categories, it collapses the internal engineering teams that would otherwise *buy* those categories. Custom dev tooling, internal workflow SaaS, and mid-market project management platforms are all structurally exposed as enterprises discover they can replace bespoke software maintenance with AI-assisted generation. The disruption isn't always a flashy challenger displacing an incumbent directly; sometimes the customer simply stops needing the product class at all.
--- • **[Bloomberg Technology] Sea's Shopee cuts hundreds of developer jobs globally during AI pivot** — Shopee's move directly pressures B2B SaaS categories sold into e-commerce engineering orgs: internal developer platforms, CI/CD tooling vendors, and workflow automation layers (think incumbents like HashiCorp, PagerDuty, or Atlassian's Jira at the team level) lose seat-count as the developer headcount they're licensed per shrinks. The mechanism is substitution-by-headcount-reduction, not feature-for-feature replacement.
