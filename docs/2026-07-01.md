## 1. The Big Thing

## The Big Thing: Vertiv's Malaysia Factory Is a Physical Timestamp on the AI Infrastructure Race

Vertiv Holdings opened a manufacturing facility in Malaysia this week, and while a factory ribbon-cutting might seem like routine industrial news, you should read it as something more precise: a geopolitical and economic timestamp on where the AI infrastructure buildout actually stands. Vertiv makes the thermal management systems, power distribution units, and critical infrastructure that keep data centers running — the unglamorous hardware layer that sits between a hyperscaler's ambitions and physical reality. When a company at this tier of the supply chain commits to a permanent manufacturing footprint in Southeast Asia, it's not responding to a forecast. It's responding to signed purchase orders and capacity constraints that already exist.

The Malaysia location is deliberate, not incidental. The country has emerged as a preferred destination for data center investment across the Asia Pacific, attracting commitments from Microsoft, Google, and others who are building out regional AI serving capacity to meet latency requirements and satisfy data sovereignty pressures from governments that increasingly want their citizens' data processed locally. Malaysia offers a combination of relatively stable grid infrastructure, English-language legal frameworks, and lower land and labor costs than Singapore — which has effectively capped new data center construction due to power constraints. Vertiv's plant positions the company to serve this regional cluster without the logistics costs and lead times of shipping heavy power infrastructure from North America or Europe.

The broader implication here is that the AI infrastructure supply chain is undergoing a structural geographic expansion that will be difficult to reverse. Vertiv's move follows a pattern visible across the stack: TSMC in Arizona, NVIDIA's diversified assembly partnerships, and now power infrastructure manufacturing moving closer to end demand in Asia. This isn't just supply chain optimization — it's the physical layer of AI becoming embedded in regional economic and political relationships. For investors, this is a signal that the infrastructure capex cycle has enough duration and visibility that suppliers are willing to make decade-scale fixed asset commitments. For founders building on top of cloud AI, it suggests that Asia Pacific inference capacity is about to get meaningfully cheaper and more available.

Watch two things from here. First, whether Vertiv's Malaysia production ramp correlates with accelerated data center commissioning timelines across the region — if the thermal and power bottleneck eases, you could see hyperscalers pull forward capacity that was previously delayed. Second, watch how this plant factors into any future US export control or tariff escalation targeting AI-adjacent manufacturing. A Vertiv facility in Malaysia produces equipment that could flow to Chinese customers if controls aren't carefully structured — and that ambiguity will not go unnoticed in Washington.

## 9. Papers of the Week

## Papers of the Week

This week's preprints cluster around a shared obsession: the gap between how models are trained and how they actually behave at deployment. Whether it's tokenizers blind to the generator's needs, world models that can't adapt when their predictions drift, or LLMs that hallucinate with misplaced confidence, researchers are targeting the seams where standard pipelines break down. The theoretical side delivers too, with a long-suspected empirical truth about random reshuffling finally getting rigorous proof.


- **[arXiv] GEAR closes the tokenizer-generator feedback loop in autoregressive image synthesis** — Standard VQ pipelines freeze the tokenizer before generator training begins, meaning the quantization scheme is never optimized for what the autoregressive model actually needs; GEAR trains both jointly end-to-end, directly attacking this structural blind spot. [Link](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.32039v1)

- **[arXiv] Reinforcement learning with metacognitive feedback improves LLM uncertainty calibration** — Rather than post-hoc calibration patches, this paper uses RL to train LLMs to monitor their own knowledge boundaries during inference, directly targeting the systemic overconfidence that makes hallucinations so dangerous in production systems. [Link](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.32032v1)

- **[arXiv] Cross-Space Distillation enables one-step diffusion students to learn from larger-latent-space teachers** — The shared-latent-space assumption has silently blocked knowledge transfer from high-capacity models like SD 3.5 and Flux into compact deployable models like SD 1.5; this paper introduces a distillation framework that explicitly bridges mismatched latent resolutions. [Link](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.32020v1)

- **[arXiv] AdaJEPA performs test-time adaptation of latent world models to counteract distribution shift** — Most planning systems built on latent world models fail silently when predictions drift at test time because the world model is frozen; AdaJEPA updates the model's latent predictions on-the-fly, which matters especially for real-world robotics and sim-to-real transfer scenarios. [Link](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.32026v1)

- **[arXiv] Introspective coupling shows LM self-explanations can track behavioral change even under fixed supervision** — The surprising finding here is that models trained on static counterfactual explanations still generate explanations that remain faithful as behavior evolves, which meaningfully complicates the assumption that explanation faithfulness requires dynamic supervision. [Link](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.32038v1)

- **[arXiv] Random Reshuffling is formally proven to dominate vanilla SGD** — Practitioners have long preferred random reshuffling over true i.i.d. sampling on intuition and empirical results alone; this paper provides theoretical guarantees that RR strictly outperforms SGD, closing a foundational gap in optimization theory with direct implications for how training convergence proofs should be written going forward. [Link](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.32005v1)
