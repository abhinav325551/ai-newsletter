## THE BIG THING

### Pictet's 30% AI Allocation Signals Institutional Risk Appetite Is Back — and It's Concentrated

A $3.5 billion multi-asset fund at Pictet Asset Management has rotated as much as 30% of its cash-equivalent holdings directly into AI equities across the US and Asia, according to Bloomberg. This isn't a marginal rebalancing — shifting 30% of cash reserves into a single thematic bet is an aggressive posture for a fund of this profile. Pictet is framing it explicitly as a "risk revival," which tells you something important: institutional money that had been sitting defensively on the sidelines is now making a directional call that the AI trade has durable legs, not just narrative momentum.

The context here matters. Over the past 18 months, a meaningful cohort of institutional allocators had grown cautious on AI-exposed equities after the 2023–2024 valuation surge, questioning whether capital expenditure from hyperscalers would translate into proportional revenue growth across the stack. The fact that Pictet is now moving decisively — and spreading exposure across both Asian and US markets — suggests the fund's thesis has shifted: either the revenue conversion story is becoming more legible, or the competitive dynamics between US and Asian AI infrastructure players have matured enough to justify a geographically diversified long position. The Asia component is particularly worth noting, as it implies exposure to names outside the familiar Magnificent Seven concentration that has defined most retail and passive AI exposure.

For you watching the broader AI investment landscape, this move is a leading indicator worth tracking carefully. When a multi-asset fund — not a dedicated tech fund, but one managing across asset classes with an explicit mandate to balance risk — starts treating AI equities as the primary vehicle for a risk-on rotation, it reflects a structural reassessment rather than opportunistic trading. Other multi-asset managers will be watching Pictet's positioning. If this allocation performs through Q2 and Q3, expect the reallocation to become a template. The more consequential downstream effect is on valuations: concentrated institutional inflows into a relatively narrow set of AI-correlated names tends to compress the entry windows for later movers and further bifurcates performance between perceived AI infrastructure winners and everything else in the tech stack.

What to watch next: Look for whether other European and Asian multi-asset managers follow Pictet's lead in the coming two quarterly rebalancing cycles — that would confirm this as a trend rather than an outlier call. Pay attention to which specific Asian AI names are receiving inflows, as that will clarify whether institutional capital is betting on the US-China AI infrastructure race as a two-horse dynamic or backing specific semiconductor, data center, or model-layer companies in markets like Japan, South Korea, or Taiwan. Also watch hyperscaler earnings guidance closely — Pictet's thesis is implicitly dependent on capex-to-revenue conversion finally showing up in the numbers. If it doesn't, a 30% cash-to-equity rotation can reverse as fast as it was initiated.
