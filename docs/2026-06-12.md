## 9. Papers of the Week

## Papers of the Week

This week's preprints cluster around a shared obsession: squeezing more geometric and structural fidelity out of generative models without adding proportional data or architectural complexity. Several papers attack the same underlying problem — that reconstruction-oriented training objectives are a poor proxy for the geometric understanding, action grounding, or scientific reasoning we actually want — and propose targeted post-training recipes as the fix. The throughline is efficiency: doing more with less labeled data, fewer architectural changes, and sparser parameter updates.


- **[arXiv] Modality Forcing enables depth prediction from T2I models without dense depth supervision** — The proposed post-training recipe leverages existing spatial priors in text-to-image models for joint image and depth generation, explicitly targeting the bottleneck of dense depth annotation requirements that plagued prior adaptation approaches. [Link](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13676v1)

- **[arXiv] RepWAM replaces reconstruction-oriented video tokenizers with representation-centric ones for world action modeling** — By decoupling visual fidelity from instruction-following dynamics, RepWAM argues that pixel reconstruction is the wrong training signal for models that need to learn how actions causally connect observations — a fundamental critique of the dominant video generation pipeline. [Link](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13674v1)

- **[arXiv] LLMs can automate reproducibility assessments across N=76 social and behavioral science studies** — This is a direct scalability argument against the status quo of resource-intensive manual reanalysis; the paper's quantified sample size makes it one of the more empirically grounded LLM-as-evaluator claims to date. [Link](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13670v1)

- **[arXiv] On-policy distillation updates are geometrically sparse despite dense teacher supervision** — Across multiple language and vision-language model pairs, the analysis finds that OPD-style training modifies a surprisingly small fraction of parameters, raising pointed questions about whether the method's gains are as broadly distributed as assumed. [Link](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13657v1)
